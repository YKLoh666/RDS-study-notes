Topic modelling depends on the word frequency, word relationship, and word co-occurrence in the text corpus. Correcting the variations helps to reduce vocabulary and sparsity so that the model can better capture the underlying topics in the text, avoiding texts of similar topic being considered as different topics.

b)

Topic modelling does not require prelabeling the text with the exact topic, and it also does not receive explicit instructions on what the topics are. Topic modelling discover the statistical patterns in the text corpus based on the word frequency, word relationship, and word co-occurrence, and then group the words into topics based on these patterns.

c)

- Assume that the document is generated with a distribution of topics. Violating this assumption, such as when documents are extremely short (like tweets) and realistically contain only a single topic, forces the model to artificially distribute fractional topic weights where they don't belong. This creates noisy, uninformative topic mixtures and leads to inaccurate topic assignments across the corpus.
- Assume that each topic is generated with a distribution of words. Violating this assumption, such as when the model is trained on a dataset where the word order is not preserved or where there are heavy semantic correlations (like phrases or idioms), means the model cannot capture context.
